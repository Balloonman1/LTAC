Guidelines

What the law allows, and model program language for cities writing or revising their lodging tax guidelines.

What hotel tax dollars can legally fund

The rules are state law, not local preference. Bring this to the committee when a jurisdiction says it only funds nonprofits: the statute sets the use, not the applicant's tax status.

Permitted uses, RCW 67.28.1816(1)

  • Tourism marketing
  • Marketing and operations of special events and festivals designed to attract tourists (operations were added by the Legislature in 2007; an older 2006 AG opinion limiting events to marketing only predates that change)
  • Operations and capital costs of tourism-related facilities owned or operated by a municipality or public facilities district
  • Operations of tourism-related facilities owned or operated by 501(c)(3) or 501(c)(6) nonprofits

Who can apply

MRSC, asked whether for-profit entities can apply for lodging tax funds: "Yes. The statute does not explicitly prohibit for-profit entities from applying."

  • The statute keys eligibility to the activity (tourism marketing, a tourist-drawing event, a tourism facility), not to the applicant's legal form.
  • Nonprofit status matters in exactly one place: lodging tax may pay operations of a tourism facility only if a public body or a 501(c)(3)/(c)(6) owns or runs it. Events and marketing carry no such limit.
  • "Nonprofits only" rules are local policy choices made by the council or the LTAC. They can be changed by the same bodies.

What every applicant must show, RCW 67.28.1816(2)

  • An estimate of how the funds will increase overnight stays in paid lodging, visitors who travel 50+ miles, and visitors from another state or country, plus the tourism-related economic benefit
  • A post-event report comparing actual numbers to the estimate, which the jurisdiction files with JLARC and shares with its LTAC and the public
  • Payment after the fact on documented costs (reimbursement), usually under a written contract that names deliverables, which is how jurisdictions avoid a gift of public funds

How the decision works, RCW 67.28.1817

  • Every jurisdiction of 5,000+ with the tax must have an LTAC: an elected chair, at least two lodging-business members, and at least two members from activities the tax funds, in equal numbers
  • The LTAC recommends a list of recipients and amounts; the council may fund some, all or none of that list but cannot add anyone the LTAC did not recommend, and must send changed amounts back to the LTAC for a 45-day review
  • Jurisdictions under 5,000 may take applications straight to the council
How to raise it with a committee

Lead with the statutory test, not the business structure: how many paid room nights the event books, how far attendees travel, and which hotels you work with. A for-profit event that fills every room in town meets the purpose of RCW 67.28 better than a nonprofit event that draws residents.

If the application says nonprofits only, ask the staff liaison whether that is in the city code or in the program guidelines. Guidelines are set by the council or LTAC and can be amended for the next cycle; cite MRSC's answer above and the statute's silence on applicant type.

Offer the structure the committee is used to: a reimbursement contract with deliverables, the overnight-stay estimate on the application, and the post-event report. Where the rule will not move this cycle, a nonprofit or chamber fiscal sponsor is the standard workaround (Everett, Leavenworth and Vancouver spell this out).

Model program guidelines for cities and counties

Language drawn from the better-run programs in this directory. Use it to tighten eligibility, scoring and reporting without inventing anything new.

Eligibility

  • "Applicants may be public entities, nonprofit organizations or for-profit businesses. Eligibility is determined by the activity, which must be tourism marketing, the marketing or operation of a special event or festival designed to attract tourists, or the operation of a tourism-related facility, consistent with RCW 67.28.1816." (Spokane Valley, Redmond, Lakewood and Oak Harbor use language to this effect.)
  • State the one place nonprofit status matters: operating support for a tourism facility is limited to facilities owned or operated by a public body or a 501(c)(3)/(c)(6).
  • Require a jurisdiction business registration and insurance naming the jurisdiction as additional insured at contract, not at application.

Scoring

  • Weight paid lodging nights inside the jurisdiction above all other measures; Bellevue's TPA incubator tiers awards directly by attributable room nights, and Lacey publishes a 160-point rubric.
  • Score the counting method, not just the number: a direct count (ticket ZIP codes, hotel block pickup) outranks a visual estimate.
  • Give points for shoulder-season dates, multi-day formats and out-of-region marketing spend, which are the levers that move room nights.
  • Publish the rubric with the application so applicants can self-score.

Process

  • One predictable annual window, posted a year ahead, with an applicant workshop (Kittitas County scores workshop attendance).
  • Require a reduced-award plan in every application; the committee funds down a ranked list, so partial awards are the norm.
  • Presentations: 10 minutes, required, scheduled three to four weeks after the deadline, with slides submitted with the application (Spokane Valley, Kitsap County).
  • Send the recommended list to council unchanged, or send changes back to the committee for the 45-day review RCW 67.28.1817 requires.

Contracts and reporting

  • Reimbursement on documented costs under a written agreement with deliverables, which is how a grant to a private entity avoids being a gift of public funds.
  • Require the post-event report in the JLARC categories (attendance, 50+ miles, out of state, paid and unpaid overnight, paid lodging nights, method) before the final reimbursement.
  • Publish the award list and the post-event results each year on the same page as the application; it is the strongest marketing the program has.
